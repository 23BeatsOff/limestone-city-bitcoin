Owning Your Keys

A Guide to Self-Custody with Liana

In Bitcoin 101, we ended on a simple but radical idea: if you own your keys, no one can freeze your wealth or quietly inflate it away. But that sentence hides the question that stops many Canadians before they ever start: what does "owning your keys" actually mean — and what happens if I lose them?

This guide answers both. It walks through self-custody — holding your own Bitcoin — using Liana, a free, open-source wallet built specifically to remove the scariest part of going it alone: losing access to your own money.

Why Bitcoin-only? Liana is a Bitcoin-only wallet — by design. Wallets that also support hundreds of altcoins (like Trezor and Ledger) carry far more code and far more ways to lose funds, from blind-signing smart-contract transactions to supply-chain hacks. Doing one thing well is what keeps your Bitcoin safe. Why this matters →

1. Self-Custody: Actually Holding Your Bitcoin

When you buy Bitcoin on an exchange and leave it there, you don't really hold Bitcoin. You hold an IOU. The exchange holds the keys, and you're trusting them not to freeze your account, get hacked, or go under. Canadians learned this the hard way with QuadrigaCX, where roughly $190 million CAD vanished when the founder died holding the only keys — and again, globally, when FTX collapsed in 2022.

Self-custody means you hold the keys — a secret recovery phrase (a list of words), ideally protected by a hardware wallet. No company sits between you and your money. As the saying goes: not your keys, not your coins.

2. The Single Point of Failure

Traditional self-custody hands you a single 12- or 24-word recovery phrase (a "seed"). That phrase is your money. And there's the catch:

  • Lose it — through fire, flood, a misplaced note, or a faulty memory — and your Bitcoin is gone forever. There is no "forgot password" button.
  • Someone finds it and they can take everything, instantly and irreversibly.

That all-or-nothing weight is real, and it's exactly why so many people leave their coins on an exchange despite the risks. Liana was built to fix this.

3. What Makes Liana Different: Recovery Paths

Liana is made by Wizardsardine, a Bitcoin security company. It's free, open-source, runs on desktop and mobile, and — crucially — it lets your wallet have spending rules that change over time, written directly into Bitcoin itself (a technology called Miniscript).

Here's the idea in plain English:

  • Day to day, your primary key controls your Bitcoin, just like any wallet.
  • You also set up a recovery key that only becomes usable after a waiting period you choose — say, three or six months of the coins sitting untouched.
  • As long as you stay active (Liana reminds you to periodically "refresh" your coins, which resets the timer), the recovery path stays dormant.
  • If you ever lose your primary key, you simply wait out the timelock and recover everything with your backup key.

The result: no single point of failure. One lost key no longer means lost coins. And because these rules are enforced by the Bitcoin network — not by Wizardsardine — your recovery plan keeps working even if the company disappears tomorrow.

4. Passing Bitcoin On: A Built-In Inheritance Plan

The same mechanism solves a problem most people never plan for: what happens to your Bitcoin when you're gone? Bitcoin lost to death is lost permanently — there's no bank to call and no estate office that can recover it.

With Liana, you can give a recovery key (or sealed instructions for one) to a family member, a trusted friend, or an estate lawyer. While you're alive and active, only you can spend — the recovery clock keeps resetting, so your heirs can't touch it. But if your wallet goes untouched for the full timelock period, your chosen person can recover the funds.

You never have to hand over control while you're alive, and you never have to trust a custodian. It turns "I hope my family can find and figure out my seed phrase" into an enforceable plan.

A note on inheritance: this is powerful technology, not legal advice. Coordinate your Bitcoin recovery plan with your broader estate planning, and make sure your heirs know the plan exists and how to follow it.

5. Is Liana Right for You?

Liana shines for anyone who wants resilient, long-term self-custody or an inheritance plan — savings you intend to hold and protect for years. It's self-custodial, free, and open-source, with reproducible builds and signed releases.

It's also a little more involved than a basic tap-to-pay mobile wallet, and it works best with a hardware wallet and (ideally) your own Bitcoin node. If you're brand new, make sure you're comfortable with the ideas in Bitcoin 101 first, then treat this as leveling up into serious self-custody.

6. What You'll Need

  • A computer or phone to run Liana (desktop or mobile).
  • At least one — ideally two — hardware wallets, so your primary and recovery keys live on separate devices kept in separate places.
  • A metal backup plate for your recovery words (paper survives neither fire nor flood).
  • Optional but recommended: your own Bitcoin node, for maximum privacy. Liana's community edition connects to your node.
  • Pen, paper, and a little patience.

7. Choosing a Hardware Wallet

A hardware wallet keeps your keys on a dedicated offline device, so they never touch your internet-connected computer. Liana supports any device that implements Miniscript. Our pick:

Coldcard (Mk4 / Q)

Open-source, Bitcoin-only, and fully air-gapped — it can sign over microSD or QR without ever plugging into your computer. Battle-tested and well-supported, with full Miniscript support for Liana. The Q model adds a full keyboard and larger screen if you want a friendlier experience. See our Coldcard Q guide.

Another Liana-supported option is Specter DIY, a build-it-yourself, air-gapped signer. See Liana's full list of supported devices →

A note for DIY fans: SeedSigner is a popular open-source, air-gapped signer, but it does not yet support Miniscript — so it can't be used with Liana's recovery setups for now.

Buy direct. Always purchase a hardware wallet from the manufacturer — never from a third-party marketplace or used. A tampered device can quietly steal your funds.

8. Setting Up Liana (The High-Level Path)

This is the shape of the process. For the exact click-by-click, follow Liana's own up-to-date guides — linked at the bottom.

  • Download from the official source only — the Liana releases page on GitHub.
  • Verify the download. Liana publishes signed binaries with reproducible builds. Check the signature and checksum before you install (more below).
  • Create your keys on your hardware wallet(s), and write the recovery words on paper, then metal — never as a digital file, screenshot, or photo.
  • Configure your recovery setup: a primary key, a recovery key, and a timelock. Pick a timelock long enough that you'll never trip it by accident (several months) but short enough that recovery isn't painful.
  • Back up your wallet descriptor alongside every seed backup (this is the big one — see the warning below).
  • Receive a small test amount first, and verify the receive address on your hardware device's own screen before sending anything.
  • Do a recovery dry run before trusting it with serious money — practice with a tiny amount so you know the recovery actually works.

⚠️ Back up your descriptor, not just your seed. A Liana wallet cannot be restored from your recovery words alone. You also need the wallet descriptor — the policy that ties your keys and timelock together. Store a copy of it with each seed backup. Without it, recovery becomes extremely difficult. This trips people up, so don't skip it.

9. The Golden Rules of Self-Custody

  • Your recovery words are the keys. Never type them into a website, an app, or a "support agent" chat. No legitimate person or wallet will ever ask for them.
  • Keep them offline. No photos, no cloud storage, no password managers, no email. Metal backup, stored privately.
  • Verify addresses on the device. Always confirm a receive address on your hardware wallet's own screen — malware can swap what you see on your computer.
  • Start small. Move meaningful funds only after a full backup-and-recovery dry run.
  • Beware impersonators. Scammers pose as wallet "support" on Telegram, X, and Discord. Limestone City Bitcoin will never DM you first or ask for your recovery phrase.
